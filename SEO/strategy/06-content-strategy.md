# Detailed content strategy

Build a small resource library that helps people prepare, organize, and make informed community-support decisions. The first collection should produce useful tools and establish editorial responsibility while the foundation confirms active services and its service area.

## Content principles

Every article needs a real reader task. It should help that reader complete something: plan a drive, choose questions for a program, scope a partnership, or find a verified source of information. A mission statement rewritten into a list article does not meet that standard.

Google's people-first guidance and its 2026 AI optimization guide support original contributions and clear purpose. They do not prescribe a magic article length, keyword density, or special AI markup. Treat the suggested lengths in briefs as effort and layout estimates, not ranking requirements. [People-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), [2026 AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

## Four editorial pillars

| Pillar | Reader task | Initial topics | Original material | Suitable next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare to volunteer | Understand fit, expectations, and questions | Before volunteering with children; mentor versus tutor | Question sheet and comparison worksheet | Related reading now; verified role later |
| Organize practical support | Turn good intentions into a feasible project | Supply-drive planning; helping without money | Needs-confirmation form, inventory, handoff checklist | Use the tool; contact a confirmed recipient |
| Build community partnerships | Define useful support without overpromising | Small-business partnership guide | Scope worksheet with responsibilities and review points | Independent planning now; maintained partner intake later |
| Navigate local resources | Find and check providers | Verified local family-support directory; choosing a mentoring program | Provider verification log and source links | Provider's current official channel; Agape only if appropriate |

The first three pillars can be developed without claiming active Agape services. The fourth requires country-specific or local verification and maintenance.

## Topic selection and sequence

### First four commissions

1. A school supply drive planning guide with an HTML checklist and printable worksheet.
2. Questions to ask before volunteering with children, with a safeguarding reviewer.
3. Ways to support families without donating money, organized by time and skills.
4. A practical small-business nonprofit partnership guide with a scope worksheet.

Finish these before expanding into scholarships, workforce development, housing, accessibility law, or emergency aid. Each added subject needs its own evidence and reviewer capacity.

### Second collection

Choose the next guides using search data, reader feedback, and the time reviewers can offer. Candidates are the mentor-versus-tutor comparison, a country-specific mentoring-program finder, and an inclusive-event checklist. Publish them only when they serve a clear reader need and pass review.

### Local collection

Once geography is confirmed, build one excellent local resource page if a responsible owner can maintain it. Add official providers and explain inclusion criteria. A clear directory with a last-checked date is preferable to several neighborhood pages with identical lists.

## Reusable article structure

1. A title and opening that identify the task and expected outcome.
2. A concise answer or action summary that stands alone.
3. A list of what the reader needs to know or confirm first.
4. A logical sequence, decision table, or comparison.
5. An original worksheet, example, or tool integrated into the relevant steps.
6. Practical limitations and points where the reader should ask a provider.
7. Helpful related resources and a suitable next step.
8. Named authorship or accountable editorial owner, reviewer where relevant, publication date, and substantive review date.

Do not force FAQs, a fixed number of headings, or a 2,000-word target into every article. If the task takes 700 useful words and a worksheet, publish that rather than stretch it. If it requires more explanation, provide it.

## Original assets to create

### Supply-drive planning worksheet

Fields: recipient organization; approved contact method; items requested; items not accepted; quantities; grade or age range if relevant; collection dates; accessible drop-off arrangements; storage; transport; handoff; stock totals; final confirmation. Clearly label all sample values illustrative. Do not include a fictional Agape collection address.

The HTML guide remains the principal search destination. A downloadable version should contain the canonical guide URL and version date. If maintaining both HTML and PDF, decide whether the PDF should be indexed; do not assume both must compete for the same query.

### Volunteer question sheet

Prompts: role boundaries; time commitment; required checks; orientation and training; supervision; reporting concerns; access needs; transport; costs or reimbursements; communication with families; changes or withdrawal. A relevant reviewer must check phrasing. This is a preparation tool, not an application form or a statement of Agape policy.

### Partnership scope worksheet

Fields: community need; partner-provided resource; staff time; budget or in-kind support; named owners; delivery dates; privacy and photography expectations; permitted public references; success measure; review date. Keep gift and tax questions directed to the appropriate responsible party.

### Comparison worksheet

For mentor versus tutor: goal; subject-specific support; relationship focus; qualifications and supervision to ask about; time commitment; program limitations; family questions. Do not make a diagnosis or imply every provider follows the same model.

## First-hand material to collect

The foundation's original contribution should grow as its work develops:

- An approved founder interview about the current mission and how priorities are chosen.
- A documented planning decision, including what the foundation chose not to offer and why.
- A provider or partner interview, with permission to identify the organization and link to it.
- A reviewed example worksheet that solves a real planning problem without exposing personal details.
- After actual delivery, an aggregate project update with accurate dates, scope, records, and limitations.

Do not invent an experience to make an article appear authoritative. When an example is hypothetical, say so next to the example. When evidence is absent, choose a different angle or wait.

## Voice and family representation

Write in clear, respectful English. Refer to children, families, caregivers, and people with disabilities without pity, stereotypes, or assumptions about family structure. Respect an individual's language and identification preferences. Ask before sharing personal stories or images, and minimize identifying information about minors.

Avoid unsupported words such as best, leading, proven, transformative, and guaranteed. Prefer concrete descriptions of the actual task or service. Use active sentences and define unfamiliar terms once.

## Authorship and review

Proposed roles are not assigned staff positions until the foundation names owners:

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Foundation decision owner | Approves mission, organizational facts, service availability, and permission to identify people or partners |
| Editorial lead | Owns scope, audience, sourcing, originality, readability, and updates |
| Subject reviewer | Reviews safeguarding, education, accessibility, or local-service details where needed |
| Developer | Implements publishing, metadata, internal links, structured data, and technical checks |
| Measurement owner | Defines events, checks data quality, and reports outcomes with limitations |

One person may cover several roles, but a writer should not imply expert review when none occurred.

## Research standards

Use current official provider pages for operational details, recognized subject organizations for general practices, and the foundation's own approved records for Agape facts. Search snippets help discover sources; verify the actual source before relying on it.

A reference to MENTOR can support a discussion about program preparation. It cannot establish Agape's screening policy. A school supply list can show that requested items vary. It cannot establish what Agape accepts. A charity registry entry for another Agape entity cannot establish this foundation's registration.

AI can help organize research and drafts, but a human owner must verify the sources, facts, original tool, and finished copy. Do not generate a large set of near-identical articles or invent firsthand experience. [Google's guidance on AI-assisted content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content).

## Production workflow

1. Select a task from the keyword map and confirm its publication gates.
2. Check for an existing page that already answers it.
3. Review current search results in the confirmed market and document the intended content type.
4. Interview the responsible person or collect the original tool input.
5. Draft from a brief with sources and the original contribution identified.
6. Run readability and metadata reviews.
7. Review the six content SEO criteria: intent, natural topic language, experience and trust, helpfulness, freshness, and internal links.
8. Obtain the factual and specialist reviews appropriate to the topic.
9. Implement and check the actual page on desktop and mobile.
10. Publish only complete approved material, add discovery links, and record the next review date.

The [publishing checklist](../templates/publishing-checklist.md) is the release record. It is not a substitute for the subject review.

## Publishing capacity

For the standard plan, expect approximately one finished resource every one to two weeks after initial technical and factual work. Some weeks should refresh a core page or improve an existing guide rather than add a URL. The 12-week calendar includes these non-publication weeks deliberately.

If the safeguarding or accessibility reviewer is unavailable, move that brief back. Substitute a lower-risk planning resource with its own complete review rather than remove the review requirement.

## Distribution and reuse

For each complete resource, prepare a short summary, a useful excerpt of the tool, and a direct link to the canonical page. Potential channels include approved organizational social profiles, newsletters that actually exist, a relevant school or community organization's resource page, and a real partner's communication channels.

This plan does not authorize sending outreach, creating accounts, or claiming partnerships. The user requested planning. The authority plan specifies what to prepare and how later outreach should be evaluated.

## Review and retirement

Review general evergreen guides at least quarterly as an operational starting point. Review local directories and current role details monthly, and immediately after a reported change. These are proposed maintenance cadences; reduce the content scope if the team cannot support them.

Use three decisions: improve, retain, or merge/retire. Improve a useful page with a specific missing answer. Retain a complete page without rewriting it merely to change its date. Merge substantially overlapping pages with a redirect plan. Retire inaccurate service information promptly, preserving a helpful current explanation where appropriate.

## Measures of content quality

A commissioned page should have a clear task, an original contribution, an accountable owner, current sources, meaningful discovery links, a suitable next step, and no unsupported claims. Track these alongside search impressions and visitor actions. Publishing quantity alone is not a quality measure.
