# Local visibility and authority development

The local SEO branch stays conditional until the foundation confirms its service area and operational model. A location token in a keyword spreadsheet is not a reason to create a location page, directory listing, map profile, or office address.

## Facts required for local work

Confirm the service country, region, city or counties, whether help is direct or referred, whether the foundation meets people in person, whether an office is public, maintained hours if relevant, and the appropriate public contact route. Distinguish a mailing address from a visitor-facing location.

The foundation may serve one location and accept support from elsewhere. These are different audiences. A donor's location should not automatically become a claimed service area.

Do not advertise proximity with `near me` repeated in prose. A clear real location and supported service are the useful information. The research did not establish local keyword volume or competition for any specific city.

## Google Business Profile eligibility

Do not create a profile solely because Agape is a foundation with a website. Google's policy generally requires eligible in-person contact, and identifies online-only organizations as ineligible. Confirm the actual delivery model and current policy before claiming or changing a profile. [Google eligibility overview](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416), [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

If eligible, use the real organization name, the correct category for the actual work, an accurate address or service-area configuration, maintained hours, approved photographs, and a working contact route. No keyword additions to the business name, virtual offices presented as staffed sites, or profiles for unstaffed locations.

If not eligible, pursue accurate organizational identity through the website, appropriate directories, approved social profiles, and genuine community references. Lack of map eligibility does not prevent normal website SEO.

## Directory and citation strategy

Make a verified master record before creating entries:

- Organization name and any approved alternate name.
- Canonical website and public contact method.
- Actual geography and operational status.
- Accurate mission descriptor.
- Registration or charity information only where established and relevant.
- Responsible person and review date.

Potential directory types include the actual local volunteer center, an applicable community resource network, school or library resource lists, a chamber or business association where membership is real, and a charity registry appropriate to the country and status. In a confirmed United States market, [211's local-service locator](https://register.211.org/) is one starting point to investigate; neither listing nor eligibility is assumed.

Quality and relevance matter more than directory count. Keep a record of the entry URL, approved data, account owner, date submitted, publication status, and correction process. Do not buy a bulk package of unrelated listings.

## One local resource directory before many location pages

Start with one location where the foundation has a real connection and a person who can verify information. A useful directory should state its scope and explain that providers decide availability and eligibility.

For each provider record, collect:

| Field | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Official provider name and website | Avoid confusion and copied third-party details |
| Relevant service and audience | Help a family decide whether to continue |
| Actual geographic coverage | Avoid sending people outside the service area |
| Provider's published application/contact route | Do not collect information on the provider's behalf |
| Availability or eligibility caveat from the provider | Avoid implying guaranteed help |
| Last checked date and source | Make maintenance accountable |
| Correction and removal route | Respond to changes and errors |

Link to the current official provider page rather than copying long changing lists of requirements. Review operational details monthly as a starting cadence and whenever a change is reported. If the foundation cannot maintain the directory, reduce its scope or link to a maintained official source instead.

## Earning relevant references and links

The strongest initial outreach proposition is a useful asset or a genuine relationship, not a request for an SEO link. Prepare a specific reason a school, library, volunteer center, business group, or partner might want to reference the resource.

| Asset or relationship | Potential audience | Appropriate request later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needs-first school-supply planning tool | School staff, approved education partners, community organizers | Consider adding it to an appropriate planning resource list |
| Volunteer preparation question sheet | Volunteer centers and youth-serving organizations | Review the tool or share it where it helps prospective volunteers |
| Small-business partnership worksheet | Local business networks and actual partners | Use the worksheet for a real project conversation |
| Documented partnership or activity | Organization that actually participated | Publish accurate attribution and link to the relevant project page |
| Approved founder interview | Relevant local media or community publication | Discuss the actual mission and launch scope |

No outreach has been sent. No affiliation is claimed by including a prospect type in this plan.

## Outreach process

1. Confirm the relationship or relevance and the recipient's appropriate public channel.
2. Check whether the resource is already listed or whether contact would be useful.
3. Prepare a short, specific explanation of the reader value.
4. Obtain the user's authorization before sending messages through tools.
5. Record actual responses and accepted references without inventing outcomes.
6. Thank participants and keep the resource accurate.

Avoid link exchanges, bought editorial links, mass guest-post offers, fabricated awards, and articles created only to host a backlink. Google's spam policies cover manipulative link and content practices. [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).

## Reviews and stories

Request public reviews only where appropriate, truthful, and consistent with platform policy. Do not pressure families, disclose service use, or offer incentives for positive reviews. For stories involving children or sensitive circumstances, use a consent process appropriate to the foundation's jurisdiction and responsibilities.

A statement from a real partner can support credibility if the partner approves the wording and identification. It does not establish effectiveness beyond what the statement and records support.

## Proposed monthly authority routine

- Check approved profiles and existing directory entries for consistent facts.
- Verify the most visited resource listings and correct reported errors.
- Identify a small number of genuinely relevant organizations for potential relationship development.
- Improve one useful asset based on real feedback.
- Record newly earned references and whether they bring relevant readers.

The initial suggested output is a verified prospect list and one useful asset, not a quota of backlinks. Link quantity alone does not establish credibility or impact.
