# SEO positioning and priorities

Agape's first search objective is to help the right people recognize the foundation and understand what it can currently offer. A large collection of articles cannot compensate for unclear identity, unverified program availability, or an inquiry form that cannot deliver a message.

## Organization facts that guide the strategy

The current website describes a foundation committed to underserved children and families through education, mentorship, resources, supportive programs, and community opportunity. Ten program areas describe possible initiatives. The wording explicitly makes availability dependent on resources and partnerships. That is a mission and planning scope, not evidence that ten operating programs exist.

See [the client information record](../../docs/client-information-2026-10-21.md).

Use this order of authority:

1. New factual answers and approved information from the foundation.
2. The current approved mission, current website, and repository README.
3. Program records, approved policies, and original evidence supplied by the foundation.
4. The historical brand guide for visual continuity and background only where it does not conflict with current facts.

The project timezone, developer's location, hosting account, domain suffix, photographs, and another Agape organization's address do not establish this foundation's service area.

## Three outcomes

| Outcome | Useful evidence of progress | What does not count as success |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People find the correct foundation | Full-name impressions and clicks; accurate organizational profiles; correct canonical pages | Traffic intended for another Agape organization |
| Supporters and partners learn how to contribute | Visits to relevant program and involvement pages; qualified inquiries after delivery is enabled | Clicks on a disabled form button or checkout placeholder |
| Families find accurate, usable information | Verified resource use; suitable referrals; clear current availability | Ranking for urgent help that Agape cannot provide |

A completed donation is a later outcome. It cannot be reported while checkout is unconfigured. Likewise, a message delivered to the foundation is not an available outcome while `/api/contact` reports `available: false`.

## Audience priorities

### People already aware of Agape

They may have met the founder, heard the name, or received a link. Their questions are about identity, mission, programs, credibility, and contact. The homepage and About page should answer these directly, using the full foundation name consistently.

### Potential volunteers and community partners

They need a practical role, realistic commitment, eligibility, supervision, and an actual way to express interest. Educational preparation guides can help before roles open. Local recruitment pages need verified opportunities and a working intake route.

### Families and caregivers

They need clear service boundaries and reliable directions. Their time may be limited, and inaccurate availability can cause real difficulty. A family resource guide must state who provides each service and when the information was checked. It must not position Agape as an emergency, healthcare, legal, or benefits authority without that capability.

### Donors and institutional funders

They need purpose, governance, evidence, and an approved giving method. The site should earn trust through verifiable facts. Grantmakers may use branded searches to check an organization; that does not make broad grant-advice keywords a useful acquisition strategy for Agape.

## Positioning to develop

Use a factual descriptor near the foundation's name: a community-driven foundation serving children and families in Georgia, United States. Replace abstract claims with the actual mechanism when program details become available: who receives what support, through which partner, and how to ask about it.

Do not describe the foundation as Christian, nationwide, government-funded, tax-exempt, award-winning, or a direct assistance provider unless the corresponding fact is established. A similarly named organization cannot provide evidence for this one.

## Recommended resource focus

Start with **practical community support for children and families**. Build two small collections:

- Preparing to help: volunteering questions, different role types, and the difference between mentoring and tutoring.
- Organizing useful support: a supply-drive planning guide, a needs-first donation checklist, and a small-business partnership worksheet.

These subjects fit the mission while allowing the foundation to contribute useful tools without claiming active services. Each page still needs a reason to exist beyond traffic: a reviewed decision tool, a reusable worksheet, an original interview, or a specific process the reader can use.

Family resource navigation becomes the third collection when a real service market and a reviewer are available. Do not launch ten loosely related collections at once.

## Publication gates

| Gate | Required evidence | Work that waits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G1 Identity and geography | Public brand and Georgia, United States confirmed; exact legal identity, local coverage, and responsible organizational contact still open | State/country copy and service-area schema can proceed; specific local targeting and listings still need their facts |
| G2 Operational service | Active or planned status, audience, delivery model, availability, and accountable program owner | Application pages and claims that Agape provides a service |
| G3 Contact readiness | Maintained public route and successful delivery check | Volunteer and partner acquisition campaigns |
| G4 Giving readiness | Approved provider, working checkout, receipts and gift terms reviewed | Donation-focused acquisition and completion reporting |
| G5 Editorial evidence | Named author or accountable editor, relevant reviewer, source check, original value | Publishing any new educational resource |
| G6 Local resource verification | Provider permission where needed, valid service details, review date, correction process | Local resource directories and referrals |

Gates are conditions on future publication, not extra permission requests for this planning task. Research, drafts, technical specifications, and honest existing brand pages can proceed now.

## Priority rules

**P0:** identity, domain decision, Search Console setup, discovery fixes, measurement definitions, and current-state honesty.

**P1:** strengthen existing pages and create the first reviewed resources. Prefer a small number of complete guides with useful tools over a higher article count.

**P2:** expand winning content and verified local or program pages. A high-value local query can become P1 after its gates open.

**P3:** later opportunities or unsuitable acquisition terms. Broad national donation lists, scholarship applications, housing aid, and medical assistance are not initial targets without suitable services and evidence.

## Expectations and effort

The first 90 days are an implementation and learning window, not a promise of page-one rankings. The proposed standard capacity is 6-10 hours per week across research, writing, review, and SEO operations, plus a separate initial developer allocation of roughly 12-20 hours for the technical backlog. These are planning estimates to be adjusted after owners review the tasks.

With less capacity, publish one resource every two weeks and finish the core-page and technical work first. With more capacity, improve source quality, tools, interviews, and distribution before increasing article count.

Google's guidance emphasizes useful content created for an intended audience. That supports this narrow starting focus; it does not provide a guaranteed word count, publishing frequency, or traffic target. [Google people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).
