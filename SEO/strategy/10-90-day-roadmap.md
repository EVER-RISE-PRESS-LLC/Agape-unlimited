# Ninety day SEO delivery roadmap

**Schedule status, 16 October 2026:** the [ten-day action plan](../daily-tasks/README.md) is now the current execution sequence at the user's request. The phases and dates below preserve the original planning reference; they are not a competing publishing schedule. Monthly measurement and ongoing maintenance still require their actual elapsed time and evidence.

Planning window: 15 October 2026 through 12 January 2027. The first calendar week begins Monday 19 October. Use 15-18 October for decisions and setup. The schedule describes deliverables and dependencies, not expected ranking dates.

## Phase 1 Establish identity and discovery

**Window:** 15 October through 1 November. **Primary objective:** make the site accurate, measurable, and ready for content.

1. Confirm geography, program status, approved organizational facts, and responsible reviewers.
2. Decide whether to retain the Workers origin or move to an approved foundation-owned domain.
3. Establish Search Console ownership and inspect core URLs.
4. Implement the sitemap and explicit robots file after domain direction is settled.
5. Add conservative Organization identity and visible breadcrumbs where appropriate.
6. Review the proposed metadata and core-page content requirements.
7. Validate the first resource's search intent and obtain the input for its original worksheet.

**Exit criteria:** discovery work is checked in production; owners and missing facts are recorded; no new service claim has been invented; the first brief has an accountable editor. If domain or service answers are delayed, complete drafts and source work while holding the dependent release.

## Phase 2 Publish the first useful collection

**Window:** 2 November through 29 November. **Primary objective:** develop a connected collection with a clear reader benefit.

- Publish the supply-drive guide and its tool when complete.
- Publish the volunteering-preparation guide only after subject review.
- Improve About, Programs, and Get Involved with approved facts and links.
- Publish the nonfinancial community-support guide and the small-business partnership guide as review capacity permits.
- Build the resource index around the available complete guides, not empty categories.
- Prepare a relevant distribution list and messages for later user-authorized outreach.

**Exit criteria:** the first resources have original assets, named ownership, functional internal links, clean metadata, mobile-readable presentation, and review dates. The plan's standard target is four complete guides, but quality and operational gates take precedence over the number.

## Phase 3 Learn and deepen

**Window:** 30 November through 27 December. **Primary objective:** improve the resources and select the next pages from evidence.

- Review the first complete measurement period where enough data exists.
- Check whether query intent matches the page and whether readers use the tools.
- Improve a specific missing answer rather than rewriting every page.
- Select the mentor comparison or program-selection guide if the relevant reviewer can support it.
- Verify one local directory only if the service area and maintenance owner are established.
- Evaluate real role and partnership landing pages if operations and contact readiness permit.

**Exit criteria:** one monthly report, a corrected keyword map, documented resource improvements, and a justified decision on each gated page. No placeholder local page should be published to satisfy the calendar.

## Phase 4 Consolidate and plan the next quarter

**Window:** 28 December through 12 January. **Primary objective:** retain what serves readers and address practical constraints.

- Review source freshness, local links, role availability, and duplicate content.
- Improve, retain, merge, or defer each resource using its purpose and data.
- Set numerical goals for the next period only if the baseline is reliable.
- Prepare the next-quarter content backlog using actual audience questions and program capacity.
- Confirm whether contact and giving connections remain deferred. Do not enable either without the approved configuration.

## Owners and planning effort

These are proposed responsibilities, not claims that the foundation has staffed positions.

| Workstream | Proposed owner | Initial effort estimate | Ongoing effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identity, facts, service boundaries | Foundation decision owner | 2-4 hours of intake and approval | Updates when facts change |
| Technical SEO release | Developer | Roughly 12-20 hours, depending on domain work | Focused release checks and issue triage |
| Research and editorial planning | SEO/editorial lead | 4-6 hours | 1-2 hours weekly |
| One substantial guide and tool | Writer/editor plus relevant reviewer | Commonly 4-8 hours; specialist review may add time | Quarterly review or earlier correction |
| Local provider directory | Local owner and editor | Variable; depends on number of verified providers | At least monthly checks if published |
| Measurement and reporting | Measurement owner | 2-4 hours after account access | Weekly triage and monthly report |

Estimates are for planning, not quotes or staff commitments. The developer allocation excludes major redesign, a new CMS, complex account recovery, or a large domain migration.

## Capacity options

### Lean capacity

At approximately 3-5 hours weekly, prioritize technical discovery, approved core facts, and one resource every two weeks. Aim for three or four well-reviewed guides across the window. Do not maintain a local directory without time to verify it.

### Standard capacity

At approximately 6-10 hours weekly across editorial and coordination, develop the first four guides, evaluate the next two or three, refresh the core pages, and report monthly. Technical implementation has its own initial allocation.

### Expanded capacity

Use additional time for interviews, better tools, source verification, accessibility, useful visual explanations, and relevant relationships before increasing publishing volume. More pages are justified only by more distinct audience tasks and evidence.

## Dependencies that can change the schedule

| Dependency | If it is unresolved | Useful work that can continue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Geography | Hold local keywords, profiles, and directories | Brand clarity, general resource research, technical specification |
| Active services | Hold applications and operational program claims | Honest hub updates, preparation guides |
| Contact readiness | Hold recruitment and partner acquisition campaigns | Reviewed resources with independent reader value |
| Donation checkout | Hold giving acquisition and transaction reporting | Honest Donate status and trust content |
| Subject reviewer | Hold specialist advice or safety-sensitive guide | Lower-risk planning tools and other approved topics |
| Search data | Avoid unsupported performance claims | Delivery checks, reader feedback, qualitative intent review |

## Completion definition

By the end of the window, people should be able to identify the correct foundation and find its useful first resources. The team should have checked search discovery, a content process it can maintain, a keyword map, and a first report. Record any work still waiting for facts or configuration.
