## Name ambiguity is the first search problem

Two clearly different organizations have established websites using a closely matching name:

| Entity seen in research | Primary website | How it describes its work | Implication for this foundation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Agape Unlimited in Kitsap County | [agapekitsap.org](https://www.agapekitsap.org/) | Treatment and supportive services in Washington | Do not import its address, history, registration, reviews, staff, or services |
| Agape Unlimited Inc. | [agapeunlimited.org](https://www.agapeunlimited.org/) | Christian medical missions and outreach | Do not infer that this foundation has its faith affiliation, location, or medical work |

These are identity conflicts in search, not proof of a relationship. Agape Unlimited Foundation should consistently use its full name, its own mission, approved profiles, and eventual geography. The goal is accurate identification; there is no need to publish comparison pages naming these organizations.

## Competitive patterns worth learning from

### Clear service and audience routes

[Big Brothers Big Sisters' involvement page](https://www.bbbs.org/get-involved/) separates volunteering, family enrollment, and giving. Agape can use the same information-design principle without adopting its eligibility, time commitments, statistics, or program model.

### A useful operational answer

[Kids In Need Foundation's Supply A Student page](https://www.kinf.org/programs/supply-a-student) explains supported ways to participate and answers event-planning questions. Agape's opportunity is to make its own eventual arrangements similarly clear, not to copy the page's service promises or numbers.

### Specialist trust

[MENTOR](https://mentoring.org/take-action/become-a-mentor/) treats preparation and program choice as substantive decisions. A new Agape article must contribute an original question sheet, decision aid, interview, or region-specific comparison rather than paraphrase existing advice.

## Content gaps to test

These are hypotheses based on the inspected patterns, not claims that competitors have never covered them:

1. A supply-drive worksheet that starts with recipient-confirmed needs, records quantities and handoff arrangements, and works for a small team.
2. A youth-volunteering question sheet that distinguishes the volunteer's commitment, program support, family consent, and safeguarding responsibilities.
3. A small-business partnership planner that records scope, staff time, responsible contacts, practical constraints, and a simple review date.
4. A family resource directory with transparent verification dates and corrections, if an owner can maintain it.

## Before treating any phrase as an opportunity

For each priority query, record the search engine, language, country, city, date, device, representative result URLs, dominant format, local results, and apparent intent. Evaluate whether Agape can answer the actual task with better evidence or a more useful tool. If the result page expects local services, a broad informational article is usually the wrong format.

Keep numeric organic difficulty separate from this qualitative review. A mixed result page does not automatically mean easy rankings. Branded terms can also be difficult to distinguish when names overlap.

## Keyword evidence labels

- `SERP-observed`: the listed query was inspected, as recorded in the query notebook. This does not imply measured demand or a fixed ranking report.
- `Hypothesis from observed parent cluster`: the parent topic has evidence; the individual variant still needs a check.
- `Brand-derived hypothesis`: a reasonable navigational variant, without measured demand.
- `Geographic hypothesis`: a template to evaluate after service-area confirmation.
- `Scope exclusion`: a phrase deliberately excluded from initial targeting; not an opportunity claim.

The keyword inventory includes all of these labels so later researchers can improve the evidence without mistaking generated ideas for measured demand.
