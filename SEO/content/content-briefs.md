# Twelve content briefs for the initial SEO program

These are commissioning briefs, not published articles or claims of active services. The first four are the initial editorial priorities. B05-B08 depend on additional topic review; B09-B12 describe future operational landing pages. Record missing answers in the root [review checklist](../../NEEDS-YOUR-REVIEW.md) rather than repeatedly asking for them in chat.

Use the [metadata proposals](../research/page-metadata.csv), [source register](../research/sources.md), and [publishing checklist](../templates/publishing-checklist.md) alongside each brief. Length ranges below are drafting estimates, not SEO requirements. Every finished page must earn its length through useful information.

## B01 Organize a school supply drive

**Primary query:** how to organize a school supply drive. **Supporting queries:** school supply drive checklist; school supply donation list; school supply drive inventory template. **Intent:** complete a practical organizing task. **URL:** `/resources/organize-school-supply-drive`. **Priority:** P1. **Suggested scope:** 1,200-1,600 words plus a worksheet.

### Reader and outcome

A community member, workplace organizer, or small team wants to plan a collection without guessing what a school or recipient needs. The reader should finish with a recipient-confirmed list, a workable schedule, clear logistics, and a handoff plan. The guide must not imply that Agape currently accepts supplies.

### Opening direction

Start with the decision that prevents waste: confirm a recipient and its current needs before buying supplies or announcing collection. Explain that the worksheet turns those answers into a practical plan. Do not open with a dramatic invented story about a child.

### Outline

1. Confirm who will receive the supplies and who can approve the list.
2. Record items requested, items not accepted, quantities, and relevant age or grade needs.
3. Set scope, dates, owners, and a realistic resource plan.
4. Choose accessible collection arrangements and storage.
5. Use the checklist for communication, collection, sorting, and inventory.
6. Confirm transport and handoff before the drive ends.
7. Share only accurate results and approved photographs.

### Original contribution

Create an HTML worksheet and a printable version with needs, quantities, owner, dates, storage, transport, and handoff fields. Add a clearly labeled illustrative example for a small team. Include a blank inventory table rather than invented prices or claims about how many children a donation amount supports.

### Research and review

Use [Kids In Need Foundation's current supply-drive page](https://www.kinf.org/how-to-help/host-a-supply-drive), its linked toolkit if accessible, and current official recipient lists as background. Verify current guidance and build an original tool. Do not copy another charity's campaign language or collection details. A person experienced with school/community logistics should review it.

### Links and next step

Inbound: Resources hub and the education area on Programs. Outbound internal: the practical-support and business-partnership guides when published. Initial next step: use the worksheet and contact the reader's chosen recipient through its own official channel. An Agape collection CTA waits for R10 and R12.

### Acceptance and review

All requested-list examples are labeled illustrative; no collection address or date is invented; the checklist works in HTML on mobile; the printable version links back to the guide. Review before relevant school-calendar campaigns and at least quarterly while promoted.

## B02 Questions before volunteering with children

**Primary query:** questions to ask before volunteering with children. **Supporting queries:** what to ask before volunteering with youth; questions to ask a youth mentoring organization. **Intent:** evaluate fit and program responsibilities. **URL:** `/resources/questions-before-volunteering-with-children`. **Priority:** P1. **Suggested scope:** 1,000-1,400 words and a question sheet.

### Reader and outcome

A prospective adult volunteer wants to choose a suitable local youth-serving organization. The observed broad query includes overseas-volunteering results, so title and opening should make the intended local-program context clear after the service market is known. The goal is informed questions, not a volunteer placement or a policy certification.

### Outline

1. Define the role and who supervises it.
2. Ask about commitment, consistency, schedule, and ending a role responsibly.
3. Ask what orientation, training, and support the organization provides.
4. Ask about required screening and safeguarding procedures.
5. Clarify boundaries, communication, consent, and how concerns are handled.
6. Discuss accessibility, transport, costs, and supported participation.
7. Compare the answers with the time and skills the volunteer can reliably offer.

### Original contribution

Provide a question sheet with three columns: question, organization's answer, and follow-up needed. Include a short fictional comparison showing how unclear expectations lead to a follow-up question, clearly labeled illustrative. Do not score whether a provider is legally safe or guarantee that a specific check prevents harm.

### Research and review

Use [MENTOR's prospective-mentor guidance](https://mentoring.org/take-action/become-a-mentor/) and its [screening resource](https://mentoring.org/resource/screening/) for background. A safeguarding/program reviewer must assess the finished guide for the relevant jurisdiction. Agape's actual policy can be described only after R11 is resolved.

### Links and next step

Inbound: Get Involved and Resources. Related reading: mentor versus tutor and the future program-selection guide. Initial next step: prepare questions for the actual organization. Link to Agape recruitment only when R05-R06 and R11 support a maintained route and a real role.

### Acceptance and review

Named accountable editor and reviewer; no invented checks or mandatory legal rules; no guarantee of placement; no casual suggestion to work directly with children without the provider's process. Review quarterly and when the applicable policy context changes.

## B03 Support families without donating money

**Primary query hypothesis:** ways to support families in your community without donating money. **Supporting queries:** non-monetary ways to support a nonprofit; skills-based community volunteering. **Intent:** find a practical contribution. **URL:** `/resources/support-families-without-donating-money`. **Priority:** P1. **Suggested scope:** 900-1,200 words.

### Reader and outcome

A person wants to contribute but has limited money or an unpredictable schedule. The guide should help them identify a useful contribution that respects recipient needs, privacy, and their own limits. The exact long-tail demand remains unmeasured.

### Outline

1. Ask what is useful before offering a specific solution.
2. Match time and skills to an organization's confirmed need.
3. Compare one-time organizational tasks with ongoing commitments.
4. Consider administrative, communication, translation, logistics, or resource-research help only where requested and suitably supported.
5. Offer goods only after acceptance and transport are confirmed.
6. Protect privacy and avoid assumptions about a family's circumstances.
7. Make a small commitment that can actually be maintained.

### Original contribution

Create a time-and-skills planning table: time available, relevant skill, task to ask about, support needed, and boundaries. Label examples as possibilities, not current Agape openings. Avoid presenting childcare, transport of vulnerable people, or professional advice as easy unvetted ways to help.

### Research and review

Interview a responsible community organizer when available. Use current role descriptions from real organizations as examples only with clear attribution. A foundation editor should review representation, scope, and any suggested direct contact with families.

### Links and next step

Inbound: Resources and Get Involved. Related reading: volunteering questions and the school-drive guide. Initial next step: use the planning table, then approach a relevant organization through its supported channel. Do not require a donation to access the resource.

### Acceptance and review

Every example has a needs-first condition. No volunteer role or informal assistance arrangement is presented as automatically safe, available, or appropriate. Include realistic boundaries instead of encouraging unlimited commitment. Review quarterly.

## B04 Small business nonprofit partnerships

**Primary query hypothesis:** how small businesses can support local nonprofits. **Supporting researched phrase:** ways businesses can support local nonprofits. **Intent:** scope a practical partnership. **URL:** `/resources/small-business-nonprofit-partnerships`. **Priority:** P1. **Suggested scope:** 1,100-1,500 words with a planning worksheet.

### Reader and outcome

A business owner or small workplace team wants to help without promising a large sponsorship or an activity the organization cannot support. The outcome is a short proposal that specifies resources, responsibilities, capacity, and review points.

### Outline

1. Start with the organization's confirmed need and operational capacity.
2. Compare staff skills, a collection project, useful goods, venue support, and financial support without claiming all are accepted.
3. Estimate the business's available time and practical limits.
4. Agree responsibilities, logistics, communication, and a simple success measure.
5. Ask permission for names, logos, photographs, and public claims.
6. Prepare a one-page scope and review what happened.

### Original contribution

Provide a scope worksheet and a clearly labeled example for a small team. Include the questions an organization needs answered before it can evaluate an offer. Do not invent Agape sponsorship tiers, existing corporate partners, logos, match rates, or tax treatment.

### Research and review

[SAFE Credit Union's practitioner article](https://blog.safecu.org/how-businesses-and-you-can-support-local-nonprofits) is a useful example of business-community support. [Family House's corporate form](https://familyhouseinc.org/corporate-volunteer-application/) illustrates the operational information a real provider asks for. They are examples, not Agape partners. The foundation's partnership owner should review the proposed scope.

### Links and next step

Inbound: Get Involved and Resources. Related links: supply-drive guide and support-without-money guide. Initial next step: complete the worksheet. Link to an Agape partnership inquiry only after R05-R06 and R12 are resolved.

### Acceptance and review

Every option is conditional on need and acceptance; the worksheet is usable without creating an account; it does not request sensitive personal or payment information. Review quarterly or after the foundation changes supported arrangements.

## B05 Mentor versus tutor

**Primary query:** mentor vs tutor. **Supporting queries:** difference between mentor and tutor; does my child need a mentor or tutor. **Intent:** understand role differences and choose questions for a provider. **URL:** `/resources/mentor-vs-tutor`. **Priority:** P2. **Suggested scope:** 900-1,300 words with a comparison table.

### Reader and outcome

A parent, caregiver, or prospective volunteer wants to understand how role types can differ. The guide should support a conversation with an appropriate provider, not diagnose a child or declare a universal best option.

### Outline

1. Explain the general distinction in plain language.
2. Compare the goals and activities a provider may offer.
3. Explain why actual program models can overlap.
4. List questions about qualifications, supervision, commitments, and support.
5. Show how to discuss the young person's own goals and preferences.
6. Direct readers to a provider's actual eligibility and process.

### Original contribution

A family discussion worksheet records the goal, the young person's preferences, questions for a program, and follow-up. Use clearly hypothetical examples. Avoid grade-improvement promises, clinical claims, and a diagnostic quiz that recommends a service based on sensitive information.

### Research and review

An education or youth-program reviewer should check the definitions and boundaries. The [MENTOR resource](https://mentoring.org/take-action/become-a-mentor/) can support mentoring context, while an appropriate education source must support tutoring context. Search results for the exact phrase include education providers; review the result format in the confirmed market before finalizing the angle.

### Links and next step

Inbound: Resources and relevant Programs sections. Related: B02 preparation questions and B06 program selection. A verified Agape program link can be added when appropriate. Do not make the comparison a substitute for the actual provider's enrollment information.

### Acceptance and review

Clear distinction between general explanation and provider-specific rules; no promise about effectiveness; named reviewer; the table remains readable on a phone. Review quarterly and after material source changes.

## B06 Find a youth mentoring program

**Primary query:** how to find a youth mentor for my child. **Supporting queries:** how to choose a mentoring program; questions parents should ask a youth mentoring program. **Intent:** identify and evaluate a real provider. **URL:** `/resources/find-a-youth-mentoring-program`. **Priority:** P2. **Suggested scope:** 1,000-1,400 words.

### Reader and outcome

A family wants a supported program and a clear way to assess fit. The guide should help them prepare for provider conversations, with country-specific official directories or program channels. It must not promise Agape can make a match.

### Outline

1. Discuss goals and preferences with the young person.
2. Find official providers serving the actual area.
3. Ask about model, commitment, family involvement, supervision, and availability.
4. Read the provider's current process and requirements.
5. Keep a comparison record and ask unresolved questions.
6. Follow the provider's maintained route and check alternatives if unavailable.

### Original contribution

A provider-comparison sheet with source, area, model, questions, date checked, and follow-up. Avoid labeling a provider best, safest, or endorsed unless there is a legitimate basis and permission.

### Research and review

Use [Boys & Girls Clubs of America's guide](https://www.bgca.org/news-stories/2024/January/mentorship-matters-how-to-find-a-mentor-for-your-child/) and appropriate local official sources after R01 and R17 are resolved. United States sources do not establish applicability in another country. A qualified youth-program reviewer should check the finished article.

### Links and next step

Inbound: Resources and the mentor comparison. Related: local directory if verified; actual provider pages; an Agape program only if active. The primary next step belongs to the real provider, not an unavailable Agape intake form.

### Acceptance and review

Country and service area are explicit; provider information is current; no private child information is collected in the worksheet; no treatment or placement promise. Check links monthly while the article is promoted and review the guide quarterly.

## B07 Verified local family resource guide

**Primary query template:** family support resources in `[City]`. **Supporting queries:** community resources for families in `[County]`. **Intent:** local service navigation. **URL candidate:** `/resources/family-support-in-[city]`. **Priority:** P2 after gates. **Suggested scope:** determined by verified providers, not a minimum directory size.

### Reader and outcome

A local family needs to identify an appropriate current provider. The page should reduce confusion and make the next step clear. It is not an emergency response service or an application portal for other organizations.

### Outline

1. State the actual geography and directory scope.
2. Explain who provides the listed support and who decides availability.
3. Organize providers by the family's task, using only verified categories.
4. Show official links, relevant area, practical contact route, and last checked date.
5. Explain how to report a correction.
6. Point to maintained broader official directories where the page's scope ends.

### Original contribution

A local verification log, transparent inclusion criteria, and useful task-based organization. Do not scrape a directory, copy changing eligibility rules without maintenance, or list Agape as a provider of unconfirmed services.

### Research and review

Requires R01, R10, R18, and R21. Use provider-owned pages and direct confirmation where needed. In a confirmed US market, [211's official local-service locator](https://register.211.org/) may be relevant to evaluate; do not assume every country uses the same system.

### Links and next step

Inbound: Resources and Programs where appropriate. Outbound: the provider's actual current service page. Avoid a donation-first CTA for a visitor looking for help.

### Acceptance and review

Every entry has a source and date; geography matches; no guarantee of eligibility; responsible owner and correction route exist. Review monthly and immediately after an error report. If maintenance is not feasible, do not publish the directory.

## B08 Inclusive community event checklist

**Primary query hypothesis:** community event accessibility checklist. **Supporting queries:** how to plan an inclusive community event. **Intent:** prepare a more accessible event. **URL:** `/resources/inclusive-community-event-checklist`. **Priority:** P2. **Suggested scope:** 1,000-1,400 words and a checklist.

### Reader and outcome

A community organizer wants to identify participation needs and practical planning questions. The guide should make responsibility and follow-up clearer; it must not certify legal compliance or promise universal accessibility.

### Outline

1. Ask how participants can communicate access needs.
2. Review venue or online-platform arrangements with the responsible provider.
3. Plan readable materials and multiple ways to receive information.
4. Consider timing, breaks, sound, navigation, and supported participation.
5. Communicate confirmed arrangements clearly before the event.
6. Record unresolved items and seek specialist support where needed.

### Original contribution

A checklist with item, responsible person, verified arrangement, and follow-up. Make the HTML version usable with keyboard and assistive technology. Do not ask people to disclose diagnoses or sensitive records to use it.

### Research and review

Use [W3C's event accessibility resource](https://www.w3.org/WAI/teach-advocate/accessible-presentations/) and a relevant accessibility reviewer. Check jurisdiction-specific obligations separately. Do not label the resource ADA-compliant or legally complete merely because it cites a general checklist.

### Links and next step

Inbound: Resources, Values, and relevant involvement content. Related: partnership planning. The next step is a planning review with the venue, platform, and accessibility support appropriate to the event.

### Acceptance and review

Reviewer checks both content and the resource's own accessibility. No fictional Agape event or accommodation service is claimed. Review quarterly and when the referenced standards or event arrangements materially change.
