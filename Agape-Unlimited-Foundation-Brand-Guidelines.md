# Agape Unlimited Foundation - Brand Guidelines

Version 1.0 | 5 October 2026

A detailed strategic and visual identity system based on the mission supplied in the brief and the existing four-person cutout-style seal. New creative choices are recommendations for adoption.

## 03. Purpose, mission, and vision

Make the work understandable before making it memorable.

> Help families meet practical needs with dignity.
*Brand purpose*

### Mission statement

Agape Unlimited Foundation raises funds to help families facing disability-related financial challenges meet essential needs, including household furniture, medical visits, and transportation.

### Vision statement

A community where disability-related financial barriers do not prevent families from accessing essential support and a more stable everyday life.

### What the brand stands for

Care becomes meaningful when it responds to an actual need. The brand connects compassion with useful, clearly explained support. It speaks about people as members of a community with choices, relationships, and ambitions.

### Communicate the work in two horizons

| Immediate priorities | Long-term ambitions |
| --- | --- |
| Raise money to help cover furniture and essential household items. | Work toward providing vehicles as capacity grows. |
| Help with costs associated with medical visits. | Work toward building homes for community members. |
| Help with transportation expenses. | Expand only with suitable funding and delivery capacity. |

### Funding narrative

The foundation plans to pursue grants and government contracts to finance community support. Describe this as a strategy until particular funding agreements are confirmed.

### The meaning of Unlimited

Treat Unlimited as the ambition to keep expanding the reach of care. It does not mean unlimited budgets, guaranteed eligibility, immediate assistance for every request, or an unrestricted promise to fund every expense.

## 04. Positioning and brand promise

Warm enough for families. Clear enough for institutional funders.

### Positioning statement

For families facing disability-related financial challenges, Agape Unlimited Foundation is a community-focused nonprofit that raises funds for practical assistance. Its focus is on everyday needs that affect stability and access, with a longer-term ambition to support vehicles and homes.

> Practical care, delivered with dignity.
*Internal positioning shorthand*

### Brand promise

Listen respectfully. Explain the available support clearly. Direct resources toward meaningful needs. Communicate what has been provided accurately.

### Distinctive emphasis

Lead with the everyday usefulness of support: a household item, the cost of a medical visit, or transportation. Make the foundation recognizable through concrete language, a consistent family-centered seal, and calm, organized information.

### Message priority

| Lead with | Support with |
| --- | --- |
| The needs families identify. | The foundation's role in raising resources. |
| The practical support being considered or provided. | A clear explanation of eligibility and availability. |
| Dignity, respect, and family choice. | Transparent descriptions of the process. |
| Verified work and credible plans. | The founder's ambition for future growth. |

### Confidence without overclaiming

Use evidence when describing the foundation as effective, experienced, or trusted. Avoid unsupported rankings, invented testimonials, guaranteed outcomes, or implied government endorsement.

### Faith and affiliation

The name alone does not establish a faith-based identity. Keep this edition welcoming and mission-focused. If the founder later defines a faith affiliation, document that decision and how public-facing language will express it.

## 05. People and communication needs

Families are the center of the system, not simply the subject of fundraising.

### Primary audiences

| Audience | What the brand must make clear |
| --- | --- |
| Families and individuals seeking help | What support may be available, who it serves, how to ask, and what happens next. |
| People with disabilities and caregivers | Respect for personal preferences, accessible information, and practical ways to communicate. |
| Grantmakers and foundations | Defined needs, a realistic delivery plan, budget logic, governance, and measurable reporting. |
| Government contracting bodies | Relevant capabilities, scope, delivery controls, and accurate performance evidence. |

### Family representation

The four figures in the seal symbolize connection and support. They do not restrict assistance to a two-parent household, a particular gender arrangement, or families with exactly two children.

### Supporting audiences

| Audience | Useful emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| Community supporters | Specific needs and an approved way to contribute or get involved. |
| Referral partners | Service scope, eligibility, referral steps, and a named contact. |
| Volunteers | Clear roles, respectful conduct, and practical instructions. |
| Media and public stakeholders | A concise organizational description and verified information. |

### Do not merge every journey

A family requesting help needs a different next step from a grant officer evaluating a proposal. Give each audience a clearly labeled route while maintaining the same visual identity.

### Accessibility preference

Offer a supported alternative communication route where feasible. Ask how a person prefers to receive information. Provide a phone number, email address, or assisted process only when the foundation can actually maintain it.

## 06. Values and personality

Values should change how the organization writes, designs, and responds.

### Five proposed values

| Value | Visible behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Dignity | Use respectful language. Let people describe their own needs and identity. |
| Practical care | Explain the concrete assistance and how it relates to daily life. |
| Accountability | Distinguish plans from completed work and report verified results. |
| Inclusion | Design information for different abilities and communication needs. |
| Community | Present support as a shared effort involving families and local relationships. |

### Decision filter

Before approving a communication, ask: Does it explain a real need? Does it respect the person involved? Does it make the next step clear? Can every factual claim be supported?

### Personality expressed in practice

| Trait | How it appears |
| --- | --- |
| Compassionate | Warm, attentive wording without pity or melodrama. |
| Capable | Organized pages, specific information, and measured claims. |
| Approachable | Plain English, readable type, and obvious contact paths. |
| Hopeful | A realistic sense of possibility without guarantees. |
| Grounded | Ordinary community settings and useful, concrete examples. |

### Tone balance

Family-facing communications should feel welcoming. Funding proposals should feel methodical. Both should remain direct, respectful, and transparent.

> A family should feel understood. A funder should understand the work.
*Creative test*

## 07. Message architecture

A small set of messages, used consistently.

> Care that meets real needs.
*Recommended primary tagline*

### Tagline use

Use the tagline on awareness materials, presentation covers, and a website hero. Treat it as a separate line, not part of the circular seal. Use sentence case with a period. Do not force it into every small logo placement.

### One-line descriptor

A nonprofit raising funds for practical support for families facing disability-related financial challenges.

### Core message

Everyday expenses can become significant barriers. Agape Unlimited Foundation focuses on practical support for families, with respect for their needs and choices.

### Supporting campaign line

Practical support. Real care. Use this as a campaign headline, not as a second official tagline.

### Three message pillars

| Pillar | Useful supporting detail |
| --- | --- |
| Essential needs | Furniture, medical visit costs, and transportation expenses. |
| Family dignity | People have a voice in how their needs and stories are described. |
| Sustainable support | A funding strategy built around pursuing grants and government contracts. |

### Message order

State the need, explain the foundation's role, show available evidence, and provide one practical next step. Introduce the long-term ambition after the immediate work is clear.

### Verbal identity

Use the full name on first mention. Use the foundation in subsequent copy. Use Agape Unlimited in compact marketing layouts when the full name remains nearby. Reserve AUF for internal file labels or contexts where it has already been defined.

## 08. Brand story and descriptions

Tell one honest story at different lengths.

### Brand story

A household item, a medical visit, or a ride can be an important part of a family's day. When disability-related costs place pressure on a household, practical support can help address an immediate need. Agape Unlimited Foundation exists to raise resources for that support. The founder's longer-term ambition is to grow the work toward providing vehicles and building homes for community members. The foundation plans to pursue grants and government contracts as part of that journey.

### Short description

Agape Unlimited Foundation is a nonprofit focused on raising funds for families facing disability-related financial challenges. Its assistance priorities include furniture, medical visits, and transportation.

### Brand narrative structure

Begin with everyday needs. Introduce the foundation's practical role. Describe the longer-term ambition as a future direction. Close with an available next step, not a promise the foundation cannot yet fulfill.

### Standard organizational boilerplate

Agape Unlimited Foundation is a nonprofit organization dedicated to community support for families facing disability-related financial challenges. The foundation raises funds to help with essential needs such as furniture, medical visits, and transportation. Its founder aims to expand the initiative toward providing vehicles and building homes for community members. The organization plans to pursue grants and government contracts to support the development of this work.

### Founder introduction template

Our founder started Agape Unlimited Foundation with a practical goal: raise resources for families whose essential expenses are difficult to meet. The long-term ambition is to expand that support as funding and delivery capacity grow. Add the founder's verified name, motivation, and experience only when supplied.

### Copy boundaries

Do not add a founding year, service location, registration number, beneficiary count, award, partner, or tax-deductibility statement until it is confirmed.

## 09. Voice and tone

Plain, compassionate, specific, and honest.

### Voice rules

| Rule | Application |
| --- | --- |
| Explain the practical need | Say transportation expenses rather than transforming lives when describing a transport-related request. |
| Use active, direct wording | Say We raise funds to help families rather than Resources are mobilized for beneficiaries. |
| Keep emotional language measured | Use warm examples and personal choice rather than guilt or exaggerated urgency. |
| Use readable sentences | Prefer one main point per sentence. Explain unfamiliar terms on first use. |

### Writing mechanics

Use sentence case for headings and buttons. Keep the logo's uppercase lettering as artwork. Use short paragraphs, descriptive link labels, familiar words, and accurate dates. Avoid exclamation marks in service notices and formal funding documents.

### Tone by situation

| Situation | Tone and example |
| --- | --- |
| A family asks for help | Warm and clear: Tell us about the support you are looking for. |
| A funding proposal | Specific and evidence-led: The proposed support focuses on household items, medical visit costs, and transportation. |
| A request is pending | Calm and transparent: We are reviewing the information you shared. |
| Support is unavailable | Respectful and definite: We are unable to provide this support at this time. |
| A progress update | Factual and appreciative: Thank you for helping us address practical community needs. |

### Urgency

Use a deadline only when a real deadline exists. Explain what the deadline is for. Do not imply a medical emergency or guaranteed result to increase response.

## 10. Disability-inclusive language

Respect the person's own language and preferences.

### Foundation default

When an individual's preference is unknown, use people with disabilities or families facing disability-related financial challenges. When a person prefers identity-first language, use their choice. Person-first and identity-first preferences vary; ask when practical. [5]

### Practical wording choices

| Avoid as default | Use according to context |
| --- | --- |
| The disabled; the handicapped | People with disabilities; a person's preferred identity language. |
| Wheelchair-bound; confined to a wheelchair | Wheelchair user; a person who uses a wheelchair. |
| Suffers from; a victim of | Has; lives with; the person's own description. |
| Normal people | People without disabilities, when the comparison is relevant. |
| Helpless families; unfortunate children | Families seeking support; children and families in the community. |

### Write about financial need accurately

Describe the specific cost pressure or access barrier. Do not present disability itself as the loss of a person's dignity, value, or future. Families may include disabled adults, disabled children, caregivers, and different household structures.

### Keep individual agency visible

Describe what a person wants, does, decides, or values. Assistance recipients are not props in an organizational success story. Avoid treating an ordinary activity as inspirational solely because a disabled person is doing it.

### Consent and language review

For a named story, confirm the person's preferred name, pronouns, disability language, and which details they want shared. Ask before naming a diagnosis. Keep internal acronyms such as PWD out of general public copy.

> The cost of transportation was a barrier to attending appointments.
*Example of a specific, respectful description*

## 11. Stories, consent, and trust

Show the work without turning private hardship into spectacle.

### Recommended story structure

- Introduce the person in the way they choose, with relevant interests or context.
- Describe one practical need and its effect on everyday life.
- Explain the actual support provided, using verified details.
- Let the person describe the difference in their own words where possible.
- Close with an accurate next step or verified project update.

### Permission process

Explain where a story or photo will appear and how it may be reused. Obtain informed permission in an accessible format. Support should not depend on agreeing to publicity. For children, obtain appropriate permission and involve the child in an age-appropriate way.

### Personal details

Publish only the information needed to tell the story. Avoid sharing home addresses, medical records, private contact details, or identifiable financial documents.

### Evidence

Check every quote, date, type of assistance, and outcome against the record. Distinguish the person's experience from an outcome the foundation can measure. Do not turn an anecdote into a claim about all families.

### AI-generated and stock imagery

Generated photographs and application mockups may show a visual direction. They must not be described as real participants, testimonials, or documentary evidence. Label illustrative imagery where viewers might otherwise mistake it for the foundation's actual work.

### Reusable example structure

[Participant-approved introduction] needed [specific support]. The foundation provided [verified assistance] on [verified date]. [Approved quote or factual description of the result]. Omit any field that cannot be verified.

### This handbook's mockups

The application scene in this guide is AI-generated. It demonstrates layout, palette, and materials. Its people and objects do not document actual beneficiaries, possessions, or programs.

## 12. The primary seal

The four-person cutout family is the center of recognition.

*Visual in the PDF: Primary reference: existing complete blue-and-gold four-person family seal.*

### Selected primary artwork

Circular blue-and-gold seal with four family figures, balanced branches, an inner ring, and the wording AGAPE UNLIMITED / FOUNDATION. Use this supplied raster as the reference for the identity system.

### What must remain consistent

Keep all four heads and body silhouettes recognizable. Preserve the blue negative-space openings that define the cutout family. Keep the figure arrangement, branches, rings, and wording together as a single coordinated composition.

### Visual meaning

The family forms can be used to communicate connection, care, and support. The branches can suggest growth and community. These are proposed brand interpretations, not claims about certification or an external emblem standard.

### Primary use

Use the full seal on formal cover pages, public awareness materials, large signage, and other placements where the circular wording is comfortably readable.

### Source limitation

The current master available for this guide is a PNG with a white background. It is a visual reference, not an editable vector. Use a white placement area until an approved transparent export is made. Do not use a generated mockup as a logo master.

Choose a version for the reproduction method, not a new logo for every channel.

### Wordmark specification

Set Agape Unlimited in Montserrat Bold 700 and Foundation below in Montserrat Semibold 600. Use navy on light backgrounds and white on navy. Keep Foundation at approximately 45% of the first line's type size. Avoid extreme tracking and ensure both lines remain readable.

### Asset control

A new derivative is added to the asset folder only after it has been reviewed against the full seal. Do not independently retype the curved text, erase a family member, or invent a new branch pattern.

### Small placements today

If the full seal becomes unreadable, use the full foundation name in the recommended text layout. Keep the full seal on the document or page at an appropriate larger size when practical.

## 14. Clear space and minimum size

A detailed seal needs room and a realistic reproduction size.

*Visual in the PDF: Clear space diagram: D = outer seal diameter; x = 0.12D on each side.*

### Clear space measurement

Let D be the outside diameter of the gold rim. Reserve at least 0.12D on every side of the seal. Measure from the actual rim, not the PNG's white canvas. No text, page edge, photograph, partner logo, or decoration enters this space.

### Starting minimum sizes

| Use | Recommended minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Full seal, digital | 180 CSS px diameter at actual displayed size. |
| Full seal, print | 40 mm diameter on an ordinary printed page. |
| Full seal, embroidery | Use a reviewed simplified derivative; test at 50 mm or larger. |
| Wordmark layout | 140 CSS px or 35 mm wide; verify Foundation is readable. |
| Family symbol, once produced | 32 CSS px or 10 mm wide; check all four figures. |

### These are starting limits

Actual legibility controls. Increase size if the wording, heads, or cutouts merge. Social avatars must be tested at the smallest visible thumbnail, not only at upload size.

### Resolution

At 300 ppi, the supplied 1309 px-wide PNG supports about 111 mm of canvas width. The seal itself is slightly smaller because of the surrounding white area. Use a real vector master for larger, crisp reproduction.

## 15. Logo integrity and placement

One recognizable mark, consistently reproduced.

### Do

- Scale the complete logo proportionally.
- Place the existing PNG on a clean white area.
- Keep the complete name and all four people intact.
- Use a reviewed one-color asset for one-color reproduction.
- Check the seal at its final displayed or printed size.
- Preserve balanced branches and the original cutout structure.

### Background handling

A dark page can hold the existing logo on a white panel with appropriate clear space. Once a transparent master exists, use only approved background variants. Do not use an automatic background-removal result without checking the rim and fine negative spaces.

### Do not

- Stretch, squash, rotate, crop, or split the seal into disconnected elements.
- Change the number of people, remove a head, or close the blue cutouts.
- Replace the branches with unrelated leaves or asymmetrical freehand redraws.
- Add extra shadows, glows, outlines, or a new bevel.
- Make the seal translucent or use it as a large watermark behind body text.
- Use gold text on white simply because gold appears in the logo.
- Place the seal where it implies a government certification or endorsement.

### Positioning

For routine pages, use a consistent top-left identity area. For ceremonial covers, use a centered seal. Align supporting text to the layout grid rather than the curved edge of the seal.

## 17. Color use and contrast

Gold creates emphasis. Readability decides the text pairing.

### Calculated sRGB text combinations

| Text / background | Ratio | Normal text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Navy / White | 14.35:1 | Pass |
| Ink / Ivory | 11.87:1 | Pass |
| White / Community Blue | 6.97:1 | Pass |
| Navy / Care Gold | 6.38:1 | Pass |
| Community Blue / Soft Blue | 6.12:1 | Pass |
| Care Gold / White | 2.25:1 | Fail |
| Dark Gold #866316 / White | 5.51:1 | Pass |

### Contrast benchmark

For ordinary digital text, use at least 4.5:1. Large text may use 3:1; WCAG defines large as at least 18 pt regular or 14 pt bold. The foundation should normally use the stronger pairings above even for headings. [1]

*Visual in the PDF: Approved button pairings: white on navy; navy on Care Gold.*

### Dark Gold is functional

Use #866316 only when a gold-colored text detail must appear on a light background. Care Gold remains the decorative and accent gold. Do not introduce Dark Gold as a second dominant visual palette.

### Buttons and links

Use white text on navy or blue, or navy text on Care Gold. Underline links in long-form copy. Distinguish errors, success, and selection with words or icons as well as color.

### UI boundaries

Visible control outlines, essential icons, and other visual information needed to understand controls should meet the relevant 3:1 non-text contrast requirement against adjacent colors. Use navy borders on light surfaces. [2]

### Testing

Ratios apply to the specified solid sRGB colors. Recheck transparency, hover/focus states, photography, gradients, and print output separately. Do not treat a passing palette as complete accessibility compliance.

## 18. Gradients and vector production

Preserve the seal's detail while keeping everyday layouts simple.

### Primary seal treatment

The glossy gold and layered blue belong to the existing full-color seal. Use them inside that artwork. Keep body copy, buttons, tables, and most backgrounds flat so the identity remains calm and useful.

### Recommended controlled gradient

For a large optional accent, use a simple navy-to-blue transition: #082A52 at 0% and #1559A6 at 100%, oriented at 135 degrees. For a supporting gold fill, use #A7751C / #F1D987 / #D7A53B at 0% / 45% / 100%. These are new supporting treatments, not replacement recipes for the existing logo.

### Restraint

Use one supporting gradient area per composition at most. Do not fill paragraphs or small icons with a gradient. Keep text on a stable high-contrast surface.

### Native vector build specification

- Trace the approved four heads and body shapes as clean closed paths; retain the blue gaps.
- Keep the family group centered and all four figures readable at minimum size.
- Use paired branch construction to preserve matching count, scale, and balance.
- Use concentric, accurately aligned rings and controlled curved lettering.
- Keep a small, named set of gradients with editable stops and consistent direction.
- Remove stray points, open paths, and unnecessary raster effects.
- Build full-color, flat two-color, one-color, and reversed production versions from the same geometry.

### Export checks

Maintain an editable source with live geometry; make a distribution copy with type outlined. Deliver SVG and PDF vectors plus transparent PNG exports. A PNG wrapped in an SVG is not a vector reconstruction. Proof gradients in the actual print process before a large run.

## 19. Typography

A modern, readable system around an expressive seal.

*Visual in the PDF: Heading specimen: Montserrat Bold. Body specimen: Source Sans 3 Regular.*

### Heading family: Montserrat

Use Semibold 600 and Bold 700 for titles, headings, campaign lines, and the recommended wordmark layout. Its geometric structure complements the circular seal while giving communication a contemporary, organized feel.

### Body family: Source Sans 3

Use Regular 400 for paragraphs and Semibold 600 for labels, emphasis, and table headers. Use this family for forms, longer explanations, proposals, and reports.

### The lettering inside the seal

Treat the curved serif lettering as fixed artwork. Do not guess its font or retype it in a similar family. The body and heading font choices do not alter the official seal.

### Type roles

| Role | Family / weight |
| --- | --- |
| Major heading | Montserrat Bold 700 |
| Section heading | Montserrat Semibold 600 |
| Paragraph | Source Sans 3 Regular 400 |
| Label / emphasis | Source Sans 3 Semibold 600 |
| Data table | Source Sans 3 Regular / Semibold |
| Fallback | Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif |

### Font access

Both recommended families are available through Google Fonts and carry the SIL Open Font License. Retain their license files when redistributing font files. Confirm the actual font build and language coverage used in each project. [6][7]

### Consistency

Use these two families throughout a composition. Avoid thin weights, scripts, condensed display faces, and additional decorative fonts. Use weight, size, and spacing to create hierarchy.

## 20. Type hierarchy and readability

Make the next piece of information easy to find.

### Digital starting scale

| Role | Size / line height |
| --- | --- |
| Hero heading | 48 / 56 px desktop; 32 / 40 px mobile |
| H2 / H3 | 32 / 40 px; 24 / 32 px |
| Main body | 18 / 28 px |
| Compact body / forms | 16 / 24 px |
| Label / button | 16 / 22 px, Semibold |
| Secondary detail | 14 / 20 px; never for essential instructions |

### Reading measure

Keep ordinary text around 50-75 characters per line where feasible. Use left alignment and avoid fully justified paragraphs. Increase spacing rather than compressing text to fit. Use all caps only for short nonessential labels.

### Print starting scale

| Role | Size / leading |
| --- | --- |
| Cover or campaign title | 28-40 pt / 1.15-1.25 times size |
| Section heading | 18-24 pt / 1.2 times size |
| Standard paragraph | 11.5-12 pt / 15-17 pt |
| Family-facing handout | 12-14 pt / 17-20 pt |
| Secondary footnote | 9-10 pt only when nonessential |

### Large-print alternative

Offer a separate large-print version of family-facing materials when needed, using approximately 16-18 pt body text as a starting point. Test it with the intended readers instead of treating one size as universally accessible.

### Social graphics

At 1080 px-wide production size, start headlines around 72-96 px and important supporting copy around 36-44 px. These are design starting points. Verify readability after downscaling on an actual phone.

## 21. Layout, shapes, and icons

Structure first. Decoration only when it helps recognition.

*Visual in the PDF: Grid illustration: 12 columns for desktop design with consistent gaps.*

### Spacing system

Use an 8 px base unit for digital layouts: 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, and 64 px. Use a 4 mm base module for common print layouts. Keep alignment and repeated gaps consistent within a piece.

### Grid

Use a 12-column desktop grid and a 4-column mobile grid as design tools. A content container around 1200 px wide is a useful website starting point. Keep reading columns narrower than full-width visual sections.

### Signature curve

A restrained, shallow curve may echo the seal's supportive base. Use it as a panel edge or one divider. Keep essential text on a stable flat area and avoid repeated waves that make materials feel decorative rather than practical.

### Shape language

Favor clean rectangles, gentle corner radii, circles for emphasis, and open white space. Do not scatter detached family figures or branches as background ornaments. A graphic motif should not become a second logo.

### Icon system

Use one consistent outline icon family: simple silhouettes, rounded ends, a 24 px base grid, and approximately 2 px stroke at that size. Use navy or blue on light surfaces. Pair icons with text labels.

### Program topic icons

Household items, medical visit costs, and transportation may have distinct icons, but do not use a medical cross or hospital symbol to imply that the foundation provides clinical care. Vehicles and homes belong in clearly labeled future-goal content until those programs exist.

### Functional details

Use a visible 4-8 px radius for smaller controls and 8-16 px for cards. Underlines, labels, and outlines should carry meaning; gold accents should provide emphasis rather than indicate a required field by themselves.

## 22. Photography and art direction

Show ordinary life, community connection, and practical support.

### Subject direction

Show families and individuals as active participants in daily life: conversation, decisions, home routines, community settings, or using practical support. Include a range of ages, household structures, backgrounds, and visible and non-visible disabilities as the real community allows.

### Light and color

Use soft natural light, believable skin tones, and a warm neutral environment. Preserve realistic colors of mobility aids and medical equipment. A light navy or warm tonal treatment may support consistency, but avoid heavy blue overlays across faces.

### Composition

Use eye-level viewpoints where practical. Include context that explains the activity. Leave clean space for a headline when the intended layout needs it. Keep hands, faces, and assistive devices visible and undistorted.

### Avoid

- Staged distress, crying close-ups, or images designed to provoke guilt.
- Crops that reduce a person to their disability or medical equipment.
- A visual system in which every disabled person is shown receiving help passively.
- Using a wheelchair as shorthand for every kind of disability.
- Before-and-after images that promise a transformation not supported by evidence.
- Replacing a real participant's face or changing assistive equipment with AI.

### Production direction

Ask the participant what setting and portrayal feel appropriate. Check consent and caption details before publication. Use stock or generated work for illustrative concepts with clear sourcing; prioritize consented, verified photography for reporting the foundation's actual work.

### Alt text

Describe the relevant activity and context. For a decorative mood image, use an empty alt attribute in web delivery. Do not add a diagnosis inferred from appearance.

## 23. Accessibility standards

Accessibility is part of care, not a finishing effect.

### Digital target

Aim for WCAG 2.2 Level AA in public websites and digital services. This handbook gives selected design rules, not a certification of conformance. Evaluate the final interface, content, and assistive-technology behavior. [4]

### Visual and interaction requirements

- Use the tested color pairings and check real text, focus, and error states.
- Provide visible keyboard focus, logical navigation, and meaningful labels.
- Use at least 24 x 24 CSS px pointer targets or satisfy an applicable WCAG exception. [3]
- Prefer 44 x 44 CSS px controls with comfortable spacing as the foundation's usability target.
- Never rely on color, location, or an unlabeled icon alone for instructions.
- Use live text rather than embedding essential information only in images.

### Content and document requirements

- Provide captions for video and a useful transcript where appropriate.
- Describe meaningful images with relevant text alternatives.
- Use real heading structure, logical reading order, and labeled form fields.
- State errors in plain language and explain how to correct them.
- Check zoom, text resizing, mobile reflow, and keyboard-only use.
- Provide accessible HTML or an appropriately tagged, checked PDF for important public documents.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences; avoid autoplay audio and unnecessary flashing.

### Release check

Ask disabled users or accessibility reviewers to test key journeys such as finding support information and completing a request. An automated checker alone cannot establish complete accessibility.

### Format note

This designed handbook is accompanied by editable Markdown. When adapting it into public service materials, create the required accessible HTML or tagged-PDF version and verify the reading order.

## 25. Social system and campaign layouts

One useful idea and one next step per post.

### Production canvases

Use 1080 x 1350 px for a portrait post, 1080 x 1080 px for a square post, and 1080 x 1920 px for a story or vertical video. These are recommended working canvases; check the destination channel's current crop and interface before publication.

### Safe layout

For 1080 px-wide designs, start with 80 px side margins. For vertical stories, keep critical information away from the top and bottom interface regions; start with 250 px clearance and preview the actual platform.

### Three reusable post types

| Type | Layout / content |
| --- | --- |
| Awareness | Large navy headline on ivory; one gold accent; short practical description. |
| Explainer | Question or need first; 2-3 concise points; one clear next step. |
| Verified work update | Consented real photo; date or reporting period; accurate assistance detail. |

### Caption structure

Opening need or useful question; foundation's role; verified context; one action. Write the essential information in the caption too. Do not depend on tiny text inside the image.

### Logo presence

Use one readable identity area. Place the full seal at 180 displayed px or more if the design supports it; otherwise use the text layout. Do not use a full seal at a small thumbnail size simply to fill a corner.

### Content categories

Rotate mission explainers, practical assistance topics, verified progress, community relationships, and clearly labeled future goals. Use fundraising appeals only when the contribution route and campaign details are approved.

## 26. Funding and partnership communication

Build the case for support with specifics and evidence.

### Funding proposition

Present a defined community need, a practical assistance model, a realistic budget, and a method for reporting progress. The foundation's proposed grant and government-contract strategy should be described accurately at its current stage.

### Suggested proposal sequence

- Organization and mission.
- Documented community need and intended population.
- Defined assistance scope and delivery method.
- Budget, resource plan, and implementation milestones.
- Governance, delivery controls, and participant communication.
- Measurement and reporting plan.
- The specific funding or partnership request.

### Visual treatment

Use white pages, navy headings, readable data tables, and a restrained gold section marker. Prioritize the proposal's instructions and required format over decorative branding.

### Sample funding introduction

Agape Unlimited Foundation is developing a practical community support initiative for families facing disability-related financial challenges. Its immediate priorities include household furniture, medical visit costs, and transportation. The foundation is seeking suitable funding relationships to develop and deliver this work.

### Keep funding status precise

Use seeking, planning, applying, or proposed for unconfirmed work. Use funded by or supported by only when the relationship and permitted wording are confirmed.

### Public fundraising

If public donations are accepted, state the campaign purpose, how support will be used, the actual contribution route, and any relevant limitations. Use a specific example cost only when that cost has been established.

### Claims to confirm

A nonprofit description does not establish tax-deductibility, a particular registration status, grant eligibility, or government endorsement. Include those statements only from confirmed organization records and applicable requirements.

## 28. Print, apparel, and events

Choose the simplest production method that remains recognizable.

### Production applications

| Application | Recommended approach |
| --- | --- |
| Community flyer | A4 or Letter; one headline, available assistance scope, contact, and readable next step. |
| Business or contact card | Use a regional standard size; text layout or large enough seal; essential contact details only. |
| Volunteer clothing | Use a reviewed flat or one-color production asset; identify Volunteer or Staff only when accurate. |
| Event banner | Full name, a readable seal, short mission line, and one clear action. |
| Tote or useful item | One identity area and the optional tagline; avoid dense explanatory copy. |

### QR codes

Use only a real maintained destination. Leave the code's required quiet zone clear and verify a printed proof on more than one phone. Also provide a readable alternative such as a short actual URL or contact method.

### Print files

Use the printer's specified bleed and color profile; 3 mm bleed is a common starting point, not a universal requirement. Keep important content at least 5 mm inside trim and farther in if folding or binding requires it.

### Color management

Use sRGB for standard digital assets. For print, convert with the printer's actual ICC profile and inspect a proof. Generic HEX-to-CMYK values are starting approximations, not a color match. No Pantone or foil specification has been assigned in this edition.

### Gold reproduction

Care Gold is a printed color, not automatically metallic ink. Foil, metallic ink, and thread need their own approved physical samples. Keep the glossy seal for methods that can reproduce it reliably.

### Material choices

Favor durable, useful, readable materials. Mockups demonstrate the identity, not a purchasing requirement. Select paper, finishes, and quantities to suit the foundation's actual budget and purpose.

## 29. Impact and transparency

Report what happened, over a defined period, with a clear method.

### Recommended reporting measures

| Measure | Definition to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Households receiving assistance | Unique households, not repeated transactions, within the stated reporting period. |
| Assistance delivered | Type, quantity, and value of verified support. |
| Furniture / household support | Items or funded expenses, with an agreed counting method. |
| Medical visit / transport support | Funded expenses or services; distinguish assistance from medical outcomes. |
| Funding | Separate funds awarded, received, committed, and spent. |

### No invented numbers

Keep empty or unavailable measures clearly labeled. A mockup can use an empty field or a label such as Verified total to be added; it must not show a fabricated total as public impact.

### Reporting hierarchy

Show the reporting period, one primary result, category breakdowns, and a short explanation of limitations. Put methodology close enough to understand the number. Use consistent currency and distinguish households, people, visits, and transactions.

### Sample result wording

During [verified period], the foundation provided [verified assistance] to [verified count and unit]. The total is based on [defined record or method]. Replace the brackets only with confirmed records.

### Long-term goal reporting

Until vehicle or home programs are delivered, report them as planning milestones, feasibility work, or future goals. Do not include proposed homes or vehicles in completed-impact totals.

### Human experience

Pair measured results with participant-approved descriptions when useful. Do not claim that paying for a visit improved a medical condition unless that outcome can be supported and appropriately communicated.

## 30. Partners and co-branding

Clear relationships, readable names, and no implied endorsement.

*Visual in the PDF: Illustrative co-branding layout: foundation identity, clear space, accurate relationship label, then partner identity.*

### Spacing

Place partner identities in a distinct row or panel. Keep at least the foundation's minimum logo clear space between marks. Match their optical size rather than forcing every logo to the same width. Do not put a partner mark inside the foundation seal.

### Relationship labels

Use precise wording such as In partnership with, Funded by, or Event sponsor when that description is accurate and permitted. Put the label near the partner identity.

### Required rules come first

A grantmaker or contracting body may prescribe acknowledgment text, logo size, order, and placement. Follow the actual agreement. Keep the foundation's identity intact within those requirements.

### Government relationships

Do not use a government seal, flag-like badge, or official-looking certification statement to make a planned contract appear awarded. A confirmed contract does not automatically permit every agency logo or imply endorsement of the foundation.

### Joint materials

Assign responsibility for factual review and final artwork. Verify who owns a photograph and which organization may reuse it. Keep the public contact route consistent with the party actually handling requests.

### Partner social posts

Name the relationship and the particular activity. Avoid a generic row of logos without explanation. Request approved logo files rather than downloading low-resolution images from a screenshot.
